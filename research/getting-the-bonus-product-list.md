# How to Get the Higher-Bonus Product List

**Date:** 2026-08-01
**Goal:** Side income, not a business. Practical steps only.

---

## The short version

**The list you're asking for is free, official, and already sitting in your Amazon
dashboard.** It's the **Creator Connections** campaign browser, and it has native filters
for **category and commission rate**.

That's it. No scraping, no VPN, no $30–79/month tool. Brands post campaigns starting at a
**10% minimum** commission and running up to **50%**, versus the 1–4.5% base rates. New
campaigns are added **daily**.

Everything in the other two research docs was answering "how do I build a product research
system." This is the actual answer to "where's the list of high-bonus products": **Amazon
hands it to you, filterable, for free.**

---

## Step by step

### Prerequisite: you must be an approved Amazon Influencer

Creator Connections campaigns are **invisible until you're approved.** If you're not in yet,
that's the only real blocker, and no tool or list gets you around it.

- Apply through Amazon with a connected social account (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook).
- Reported 2026 approval weighting: niche consistency, posting frequency over the prior 90
  days, and a minimum of ~12 shoppable or product-adjacent posts. Follower count matters
  less than a coherent, active account.
- Rejection isn't permanent — **you can reapply.** Trevin Peterson was reportedly rejected
  initially. Post consistently in one lane for a few weeks and try again.
- You also need **Creator Stars Bronze** tier (the entry tier) for Creator Connections access.

### Then: pull the list

1. Log into the Amazon Associates / Influencer dashboard.
2. Open the **Creator Connections** tab.
3. **Filter by commission rate — sort high to low.** This is the whole trick. You're now
   looking at the highest-bonus products available to you, ranked.
4. Also filter by **category** to stay in one lane (see below).
5. **Check back daily-ish.** New campaigns post constantly and budgets get exhausted —
   campaigns have a max budget and a start/end date, so good ones disappear.

### What to actually pick off that list

In priority order:

1. **Highest commission rate you can film credibly.** A 20% campaign on a $40 product pays
   $8/sale — better than a $200 product at base rate.
2. **Campaigns offering a free sample.** Many CC campaigns include free product. That zeroes
   out your acquisition cost, which is the main thing that eats side-income margins early on.
   This is the single biggest beginner lever on the list.
3. **Things you can demo in one take.** Visible mechanism, before/after. Skip anything whose
   benefit is invisible (supplements, consumables).
4. **Brands with deep catalogs.** One campaign often covers many ASINs — film five products
   in one sitting off a single setup.
5. **Stay in one or two categories.** Consistency helps both Amazon's algorithm and your
   filming speed. Scattering across ten niches is the most common beginner mistake.

## Realistic numbers

Reported ranges, treat as rough:

| Stage | Videos | Reported monthly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | 1–10 | **$50–$300** |
| Active | 20–50 | **$300–$1,500** |

Specific reported cases: one creator earned **$320 in month one**, tracking to $1,200+ by
month two. Another earned **$968 in month one from 75 videos**, at roughly **19 hours of
total work**.

**The structural reason this suits side income:** review videos on evergreen products keep
earning for **months or years**. The work is front-loaded, the income trails. You're
building a small annuity, not trading hours for dollars — which is exactly the shape you
want if you're not trying to make it a job.

**Honest counterweights:**
- Not get-rich-quick. The $50–300 beginner band is the realistic first stop.
- Newer creators reportedly find it harder than the 2023 cohort did — more competition,
  Amazon filtering low-effort content harder.
- Payment lands **~60 days** after the end of the earning month. Month one's money shows up
  around month three. Budget for that gap.

## Do you need Trevin's tool?

**Not to start.** Be clear on what these tools actually do: they **automate access to the
same free dashboard list.** They have no secret data you can't see yourself.

| | Cost | What it buys you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Manual (dashboard)** | **$0** | The complete list, filterable. Costs you a few minutes a day of checking. |
| Viral Vue Free tier | $0 | Some automation, no card required. Fine to try. |
| Viral Vue Starter / Pro | $39 / $79 per mo | Auto-accepts campaigns by rate threshold, product research, storefront scans. |
| Oink Pro | ~$30/mo | CC auto-accept, content gap analyzer, broken-video detection. |

At side-income scale the math is simple: **a $39–79/month subscription against $50–300/month
in earnings eats most of your profit.** Automation pays off at high volume — that's the
problem it was built for (Viral Vue was built for a family running ~10,000 videos). You are
not at that scale, and won't be for a while.

**Do it manually until the subscription is obviously cheaper than your time.** Concretely:
once you're consistently past ~$500/month and checking campaigns feels like a chore, revisit.
Not before.

Also worth remembering: when Trevin recommends Viral Vue, it's his own company. He's a
credible operator, but that's vendor content.

## The whole plan, compressed

1. Get approved as an Amazon Influencer. Reapply if rejected.
2. Open Creator Connections. **Filter by commission rate, high to low.** ← *this is the list*
3. Take the highest-rate campaigns that **include a free sample** and that you can demo in
   one take.
4. Film things you already own alongside them — zero cost, builds volume fast.
5. Pick one or two categories and stay there.
6. Check the dashboard regularly; campaigns expire and new ones post daily.
7. Skip the paid tools until you're past ~$500/month.
8. Expect the first payment ~60 days after your first earning month.
