# Filming Pet Products With Your Own Dog

**Date:** 2026-08-01
**Premise:** Filming dog-product videos starring Blue.

---

## Verdict

**Good idea with one expensive flaw.** The asset is real and unusually strong. The problem
is the price point of dog toys specifically — and it's fixable without changing anything
about the setup.

---

## 1. Why this is genuinely strong

**A real dog on camera is a competitive moat.** Most Amazon influencer videos are hands-only
unboxing on a kitchen counter. A dog actually *using* the product is categorically better
content, and it's the one thing a competitor can't replicate by buying the same product.
That's rare — almost every other advantage in this program is purchasable.

Supporting evidence:

- **Emotional demonstration converts.** "Watching a dog light up when chasing a toy instantly
  builds connection." Pet content is inherently watchable in a way that most product demos
  are not.
- **Pet is a top-cited 2026 growth category** — owners keep paying for comfort, health, and
  convenience even when discretionary spending tightens. US pet toys market projected ~8.5%
  CAGR through 2030.
- **Authenticity beats reach here.** In pet specifically, micro-creators reportedly convert
  comparably to large accounts because audience trust is so strong. Follower count is not
  the barrier.
- **You appear to have domain credibility.** The `americanpuppyservices` and
  `catgroomingdirectory` context suggests real professional dog/cat handling background. On
  camera that reads as authority, and it's the thing hobbyist reviewers can't fake.
- **Zero acquisition cost on the talent.** Blue works for treats.

**Best format for this asset — the durability test.** "Blue destroys everything, here's what
survived." It's honest, it's inherently watchable, it justifies a longer video (which
reportedly performs better in this program), and it's exactly what a buyer searching tough
chew toys wants to see. It also works when the product *fails*, which most review formats
can't do.

---

## 2. The flaw: dog toys are too cheap

This is the same trap as supplements, from a different direction. Current market prices:

| Product | Typical price | Pet rate ~3% | You earn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Puzzle feeder | ~$12.99 | 3% | **$0.39/sale** |
| Popular auto ball toy | ~$20.99 | 3% | **$0.63/sale** |
| Standard chew toy | ~$15 | 3% | **$0.45/sale** |

**You cannot film your way out of that arithmetic.** A video that drives 50 sales of a $13
puzzle feeder earns about **$20**. Pet sits at the ~3% base tier, not the 4.5% Kitchen/
Automotive tier, so cheap pet products are close to the worst commission math on Amazon.

**Two fixes, and you should use both.**

### Fix 1: Creator Connections (the big one)

CC campaigns pay a **10% minimum, up to 50%.** On the same cheap products:

| Product | Price | Base 3% | CC at 15% | CC at 30% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Puzzle feeder | $12.99 | $0.39 | $1.95 | $3.90 |
| Auto ball toy | $20.99 | $0.63 | $3.15 | $6.30 |

**5–10× on identical footage.** Filter the CC dashboard to Pet Supplies, sort by commission
rate, film what's there. Pet brands are also among the most active gifters — free product is
common, which zeroes your cost. (See `getting-the-bonus-product-list.md`.)

### Fix 2: Trade up the price point — same dog, same setup

Keep Blue, keep the format, film more expensive things:

| Product type | Typical price | At 3% | Why it works on camera |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Self-cleaning litter box** | **$200–600** | **$6–18** | Highest-ticket item in pet. See §3. |
| Pet stroller / travel crate | $80–250 | $2.40–7.50 | Setup, fit, real-use demo |
| Orthopedic / large dog bed | $60–200 | $1.80–6.00 | Blue lying on it *is* the review |
| Pet camera / treat dispenser | $50–200 | $1.50–6.00 | App demo + dog reaction, two-angle content |
| Smart GPS collar | $50–150 | $1.50–4.50 | Fit on a real dog, live tracking |
| Automatic feeder | $50–150 | $1.50–4.50 | Mechanism visible, strong demo |
| **Grooming tools / vacuum kits** | **$40–150** | **$1.20–4.50** | **Direct fit with your background** |
| Durable "tough" chew toys | $20–40 | $0.60–1.20 | Best durability-test format |

One video of a $150 grooming kit ≈ **eleven** videos of a $13 puzzle feeder. Identical
filming effort.

---

## 3. The cat angle may beat the dog angle

Worth saying plainly: **`catgroomingdirectory` points at cats, and cats have better
economics than dogs here.**

**Self-cleaning litter boxes run $200–600** — the single highest-ticket mainstream pet
category, and explicitly cited as a 2026 growth product. At 3% that's **$6–18/sale**; under
a CC campaign it's substantially more. Nothing in dog toys comes close.

Cat grooming tools are also a strong fit — you'd have real professional credibility, they
demo well (visible before/after), and cat-grooming content is less saturated than dog
content.

**If you have access to both, the ideal catalog is probably:** cat products for the price
points, dog products with Blue for the watchability, both under CC campaigns.

---

## 4. What to actually do

1. **Film products you already own first.** Blue's existing toys, bowls, bed, grooming gear.
   Zero cost, immediate volume, and it tells you whether you enjoy the process before you
   spend anything.
2. **Filter CC to Pet Supplies, sort by commission rate, prioritize free samples.** This is
   the whole game at your scale — it's a 5–10× multiplier on identical work.
3. **Set a price floor of ~$25** unless there's a CC campaign attached. Below that the
   arithmetic doesn't work at base rate.
4. **Lead with durability tests and grooming demos.** Your two strongest formats — one
   because Blue is a real destructive tester, one because you have professional credibility.
5. **Work up the ladder.** Start with owned toys → mid-ticket grooming tools → beds, crates,
   feeders → litter boxes and pet tech.
6. **Stay in pet.** One lane, deep catalog. Every filming session reuses the same setup, the
   same dog, and the same credibility.

**The honest summary:** the instinct is right and the asset is better than most people
start with. Just don't spend it on $13 toys. Same dog, same effort, three-to-ten times the
price point — that's the entire adjustment.
